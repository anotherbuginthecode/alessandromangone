## Adoption is a people problem before it is a tooling problem

Buying licenses is easy. Changing how a team works every day is not. I saw it in two very different rooms: client teams who had never written a prompt, and my own engineers, who knew the tools but still wrote every line by hand.

So the program ran on two tracks. One for client teams, to make generative AI part of their daily work. One for engineering, to make coding agents part of how we ship.

## Two audiences, one goal: change how the work gets done

On the client side, a single session could hold daily AI users, people who had tried it once and people worried about their jobs. On the engineering side, the team was delivering for enterprise clients, so adoption could not slow delivery down. We needed:

- **A measured starting point.** Each team's level measured team by team, not assumed.
- **The right depth.** Plain language for non-technical people, and real depth for engineers.
- **Real work.** Exercises on the documents, decks and codebases people touch every week.
- **Clear rules.** Rules on risk, data and review, so speed never came at the cost of quality.
- **Material that lasts.** Prompts, templates, assistants and project scaffolding that outlive the sessions.

## When the training finds the product

At a film and TV production company, an adoption program for 50 people across departments surfaced the workflow worth rebuilding: how the development team goes from looking for inspiration to pitching an idea. I designed the architecture and led the team that built a platform for the whole flow. It tracks publishers' bestseller charts every day, uses LLMs to search reader reviews and synopses for books worth adapting, drafts synopses and creative briefs, then turns each brief into moodboards and social posts with image generation.

## The same rule in every room: AI does the work, people own the outcome

Whether the audience was a marketing team or a group of senior engineers, the program ended in the same place: AI handles more of the work, and people stay responsible for what it produces. For client teams that meant a method and a roadmap. For my team it meant a new default, where the board feeds the agents and the review belongs to us.
